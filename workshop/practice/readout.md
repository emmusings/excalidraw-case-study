# Practice research readout: Hover Motion

**Recommendation:** Keep Hover Motion's compact layout. Put **Image** on the rail, make **Keep tool** a labeled pin with a plain-language explanation, and give controls 44 px targets and a selected cue that doesn't rely on hue. Keep other names and shortcuts on hover and keyboard focus, with the animation off for reduced motion.

This is fictional workshop material, not Excalidraw research or a test of Hover Motion. The quotes below are verbatim from the practice files.

## 1. Repeat drawing still breaks down — 3 of 6 did not complete the task

Three of six drew five circles without returning to the toolbar. The study summary says half did not connect “Keep tool” with drawing several shapes; it does not identify all three participants.

- **P5, study:** “Keep tool... keep it where?”
- **Support specialist, feedback on Playful (not a study participant):** “The pin with 'Keep tool' under it finally explains the lock. Could the tooltip say what it does in a full sentence?”

**Counterpoint:** The other three completed the task. The pin feedback is a rationale for trying it, not evidence that it improves completion. **Confidence: high** that the task was a struggle in this exercise; **medium** that the label alone was the cause.

## 2. Image needs to stay in view — 1 of 6 missed it on tablet

Five of six added a photo. P4 couldn't see Image when the labeled toolbar scrolled; P6 found the visible button helpful.

- **P4, study:** “The labels make it wider than my screen.”
- **P6, study:** “Image being right there is great. Last time I couldn't find it at all.”

**Counterpoint:** Most participants succeeded, and the study had only one tablet participant. P6's earlier difficulty was self-reported, not a baseline measurement. **Confidence: medium** for keeping Image visible; there isn't enough evidence to estimate how common the tablet problem is.

## 3. Labels helped people get started — 6 of 6 completed boxes and arrows

All six found the box and arrow tools and finished the basic diagram. The researcher reported in the design review that **5 of 6** found every tool on their first try. Neither result isolates the effect of labels.

- **P1, study:** “Oh, they have names now. That's all I needed.”
- **P2, study:** “I'd turn the words off after a day, but today I need them.”

**Counterpoint:** P4 reported width problems, and P3 reported small targets: “My finger keeps hitting the one next to the one I want.” **Confidence: medium** that names help first use; **low** that all names should always be shown.

## Method and limits

The [study results](study-results.md) come from six first-time Excalidraw users in a remote, unmoderated exercise with a labeled prototype. The [design review](design-review-transcript.md) and [feedback](feedback.md) discuss that prototype and the Playful direction; they are not additional participants from the six. The separate **31 of 40** survey preference was for Playful, not Hover Motion. Stakeholder input sets design checks (subtle motion, reduced motion, contrast, and 44 px targets); it does not establish usability outcomes.

This exercise can surface first-use problems, not establish why people respond that way or replace talking to them. Retest repeat drawing and image discovery on the revised Hover Motion, including on a narrow tablet and with reduced motion on. Keep Original reachable for comparison.
